Coherent articulated motion + full aura composite

Two defects fixed. The animation no longer warps the sprite line-by-line, and the black dead zone between Ryan and the aura is gone — without a single approved aura pixel changing.

Why it looked sliced, and what replaced it

The old renderer displaced whole rows. A row-wise field is constant across x, so every pixel on a row moved in lockstep and the eye saw horizontal bands shearing — even though every joint was connected and torso reach measured 1.0. Connectivity was never the problem; the field's shape was.

Motion now comes from anchored joints: sixteen anchors at ankles, knees, pelvis, chest, shoulders, elbows, wrists, neck, head and hair crown, each with a gaussian influence radius. A pixel's displacement is the weighted blend of the anchors near it, so it varies with both x and y — two pixels on the same row at different x move by different amounts, which a row-wise field cannot do. Anchors below the hips carry zero displacement, pinning the feet without a hard cut-off row. The render is inverse-mapped, so it cannot contain holes.

A new slice audit detects the failure mode: shear (a row whose left and right edges move by the same amount) combined with banding (duplicated adjacent rows). Injected horizontal shear, torso, head, shoulder and hair band offsets all score 1.000; the real frames score 0.00–0.46.

The black halo

Its cause was a dilated character mask subtracted from the aura in the compositing step — added two passes ago to kill a white contour, and it over-corrected: it deleted every aura pixel behind and around the figure (5344 of 5344 for SSJ1), leaving a character-shaped dead zone that read as a black silhouette. The knockout is switched off; the stack is now transparent → full aura → lightning → Ryan.

Proof the aura is untouched: rebuilding with the knockout on reproduces the approved frozen file byte-for-byte, and turning it off alters zero of that file's pixels — palette and bounding box identical. The only change is restoring what the mask deleted.

Coherent motion, solid irises, restored hair

Three fixes, zero PixelLab generations. The loose-pieces animation is deleted and replaced by a six-frame loop that deforms the figure as one raster; every transformed iris is a single solid teal block with no pupil; and SSJ3's cropped top spikes are restored from its own raw source. The approved aura is untouched.

Why the old animation looked loose

It translated slot bitmaps independently, so each limb was a rigid tile sliding over its neighbours — held together only by an accidental pixel. A "one connected component" test passed anyway, which is why that test is no longer used as the anatomy check. The replacement drives the whole figure with two smooth row-wise fields — vertical squash/stretch and lateral sway — that change by at most one pixel between adjacent rows. A displacement that never jumps cannot open a gap, so shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips and the hairline stay welded by construction.

The anatomy test is now torso reach: the fraction of the figure reachable four-connected from the jersey. It measures 1.0000 with zero unreached pixels in every frame of every form. A limb hanging off one diagonal pixel would still be "one component" but would not be reachable.

The raw PixelLab state always contained the complete spikes; the cleanup cropped them flat against row 0. The body canvas grew from 128×160 to 128×192 and the 8 lost rows were pasted back from the raw source — no new hairstyle was drawn. Character motion and reference-derived aura

Two rejections fixed. The loops were a frozen PNG with a moving aura — Ryan now genuinely animates, articulated slot by slot. And the aura is no longer approximated with random strokes: the reference GIF's own frames are extracted and used directly.

Zero PixelLab generations. Frame 0 of every loop is the approved body byte-for-byte; frames 1–7 move owned slots — legs, hips, torso, arms, fists, head, hair — by one or two pixels. Feet never move: 0 sock or shoe pixels change in any frame of any form, so the character compresses against a fixed ground contact instead of floating.

Eyes and aura rebuild

Zero PixelLab generations. Every character is the locked artwork; the eyes were redrawn pixel by pixel inside published boxes and the aura renderer was replaced outright.

The old eyes were recoloured rather than drawn, which put teal in the lash row and left a white rectangle with a one-pixel vertical pupil. These are authored instead: a dark lash, white sclera, and a three-pixel iris block with a centred pupil. Because teal is only ever written into iris_mask, the teal rule holds by construction.

Charging aura, rebuilt

The renderer was replaced, not tuned. A flame is now a bundle of independent strokes — each with its own base, length, width, lean and curve, drawn from heavy-tailed distributions and clustered rather than spread evenly. Nothing reads the distance to the body, which is why there is no shell; the wide spread of lengths is what breaks the fence the previous three attempts kept producing.
